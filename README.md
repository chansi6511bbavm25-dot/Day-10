## LAB 10 – Aviation Image Generation and Responsible AI Use

This lab focused on understanding **AI image generation and its application in aviation management**. The activity explained the difference between image retrieval and image generation. Image retrieval means finding an existing image from a source, while image generation creates a new visual using a written prompt. The lab also highlighted that AI-generated aviation images must be reviewed because they may contain inaccurate aircraft, airport equipment, signs, people, logos, or operational details.

The lab introduced the **five main components of an effective image prompt: Subject, Medium or Style, Environment, Lighting, and Aspect Ratio**. A weak prompt such as “Create an airport image” was compared with a structured prompt describing young adult travellers using self-service check-in kiosks in a modern airport terminal. The structured prompt provided clearer instructions about photorealistic style, natural lighting, 16:9 landscape format, and restrictions on logos, personal information, and promotional text.

The activity also demonstrated **single-variable refinement**, where only one instruction was changed at a time, such as style, lighting, aspect ratio, or environment. This helped identify how individual prompt changes affected the generated image. **Prompt bleed** was also studied, showing how one instruction can unintentionally influence other parts of an image.

An aviation accuracy and professionalism audit was completed to check aircraft structure, passenger behaviour, airport equipment, walkways, signs, branding, personal information, and overall visual quality. Three aviation visuals were also planned for airline marketing, airport awareness, and an aviation presentation.

Finally, the lab covered **ethical and responsible AI use**, including avoiding unauthorised logos, misleading visuals, personal information, stereotypes, false safety information, and unauthorised representations of real people or events. Peer review and human verification were included before final publication.

Overall, this lab demonstrated that effective AI image generation requires **clear prompts, controlled refinement, aviation accuracy checks, ethical review, and human judgement** before professional use.
